## Study title

Selecting items on a screen with eye movements and an ordinary webcam

## Invitation

You are invited to take part in a short study about a communication aid for people who cannot
speak or move. Before deciding, please read this sheet and ask any questions you like.

## What is the study about?

People with conditions such as motor neurone disease may lose the ability to speak and move,
while still being able to move their eyes. Commercial eye-tracking devices exist but are
expensive. We are testing whether an ordinary laptop webcam is accurate enough to let someone
choose items on a screen by looking at them.

This session tests **the equipment and the screen design**, not any medical condition. We are
recruiting people without motor impairments for this stage, because we first need to know
whether the sensing works at all.

## What would taking part involve?

One session of about **25 minutes**, in one sitting:

- You sit in front of a laptop at a normal distance, in normal room lighting.
- The screen shows two boxes with a word in each. You will be asked to choose a named word
  by looking at it for about half a second.
- Near the start, a dot appears at five places across the screen and you look at each one in
  turn. This takes about half a minute and lets the software adjust to your camera and seating.
- You then work through four short blocks of fifteen attempts each - two ways of confirming a
  choice, each tried once with that adjustment switched on and once with it switched off, with
  a break between blocks.
- At the end there are three short questions about which design felt best.

There is no training and nothing to learn beforehand.

## What is recorded?

**No video, no photographs and no sound are recorded at any point.** The webcam image is used
by the computer as it arrives and is never saved.

What is saved is a file of numbers: which box you looked at, when, whether it matched the word
you were asked to choose, and how long each attempt took, together with measurements the
software derives from the camera image, such as head angle and eye openness. These cannot be
used to reconstruct your face or identify you.

## Are there any risks?

The main discomfort is eye strain or tiredness from looking at a screen with concentration.
The session is capped at 25 minutes and has breaks between blocks. You can stop at any moment,
for any reason or none, and you do not have to explain why.

## Are there any benefits?

Not to you directly. The results will inform the design of a communication aid for people who
cannot speak, and will be published in a scientific journal.
